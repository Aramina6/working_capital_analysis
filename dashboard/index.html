Finance primer

Full forms · formulas

Working capital is money tied up in day-to-day operations. Net Working Capital answers: “How much of our own cash is stuck in the pipeline between buying inputs and collecting from customers?”

Operating formula
Net Working Capital = Inventory + Accounts Receivable − Accounts Payable Cash Conversion Cycle (days) = Days Inventory Outstanding + Days Sales Outstanding − Days Payable Outstanding
NWC
Net Working Capital

Inventory + unpaid customer invoices − unpaid supplier bills. Primary reduction target.

CCC
Cash Conversion Cycle

How many days cash is locked from buying inputs until customers pay. Shorter is better.

DIO
Days Inventory Outstanding

Average days stock sits before sale or consumption. (Average Inventory ÷ Cost of Goods Sold) × 365.

DSO
Days Sales Outstanding

Average days after a sale until the customer or offtaker pays. (Average Receivables ÷ Revenue) × 365.

DPO
Days Payable Outstanding

Average days you take to pay suppliers. Extending carefully improves cash — without harming critical partners.

SCF
Supply Chain Finance

Bank pays suppliers early; you still pay on longer terms. Supports higher Days Payable Outstanding with supplier health.

AR / AP
Accounts Receivable / Accounts Payable

Receivable = customers owe you. Payable = you owe suppliers (a temporary cash benefit).

RM · WIP · FG
Raw Materials · Work-in-Progress · Finished Goods

Stages of manufacturing inventory. Power plants emphasize fuel and strategic spares instead of finished goods.

Cash goes out early and comes back late. Net Working Capital measures the gap. Suppliers partly finance it via Accounts Payable.

Decision frameworks

Issue tree · value tree · prioritization

1. Issue tree — where is cash trapped?

Reduce Net Working Capital
├── Inventory $ (Raw materials / Work-in-progress /
│               Finished goods · Fuel & spares)
├── Accounts Receivable $ (terms, disputes, credit, unbilled)
└── Accounts Payable $ (terms, calendar, Supply Chain Finance)

Mutually exclusive buckets under the operating definition — every dollar maps to one branch.

2. Value tree — how cash returns

Free Cash Flow
└── Δ Net Working Capital (reduction = cash in)
    ├── ↓ Days Inventory Outstanding
    ├── ↓ Days Sales Outstanding
    └── ↑ Days Payable Outstanding (guardrails)

Each initiative must map to one node and one day-metric.

Why this analysis exists

Business case
  • Cash trapped in operations — manufacturing inventory and long business-to-business credit terms.
  • Global commodities — price spikes inflate inventory dollars and cover-day habits.
  • Five-year softer revenue — static policy raises Net Working Capital % of revenue and crushes Free Cash Flow.
  • Capital allocation — leadership needs cash for Capital Expenditure, de-lever, resilience.
  • One language — Treasury, Financial Planning & Analysis, Operations, Procurement align on the Cash Conversion Cycle.
Best practice: Reduce excess Net Working Capital and shorten the Cash Conversion Cycle (lower Days Inventory Outstanding and Days Sales Outstanding; extend Days Payable Outstanding carefully via terms or Supply Chain Finance). Temporary builds only when intentional and time-boxed.

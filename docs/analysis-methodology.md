# Working Capital Analysis Methodology

## 1. Purpose

Answer four executive questions for a **global industrial** group (manufacturing with commodities; reference models for **aluminium** and **power**):

1. How much cash is trapped in **Net Working Capital**?
2. How fast does cash cycle (**Cash Conversion Cycle** in days)?
3. What happens if **revenue is flat or down over five years**?
4. Which levers release cash with acceptable reliability and supplier risk?

---

## 2. Best-practice goal: reduce Net Working Capital

| Objective | Direction | Full-form rationale |
|-----------|-----------|---------------------|
| Net Working Capital | **Reduce** excess | Free cash for Capital Expenditure, debt, resilience |
| Cash Conversion Cycle | **Shorten** | Less time capital is unproductive |
| Days Inventory Outstanding | **Reduce** (safely) | Leaner stock / spares with guardrails |
| Days Sales Outstanding | **Reduce** | Faster collections from customers or offtakers |
| Days Payable Outstanding | **Extend carefully** | Supplier credit or Supply Chain Finance — without stressing critical counterparties |

---

## 3. Core formulas (full names)

```
Net Working Capital
  = Inventory + Accounts Receivable − Accounts Payable

Days Inventory Outstanding
  = (Average Inventory / Cost of Goods Sold) × 365

Days Sales Outstanding
  = (Average Accounts Receivable / Revenue) × 365

Days Payable Outstanding
  = (Average Accounts Payable / Cost of Goods Sold) × 365

Cash Conversion Cycle
  = Days Inventory Outstanding + Days Sales Outstanding − Days Payable Outstanding

Net Working Capital % of revenue
  = Net Working Capital / Last Twelve Months Revenue
```

See **Working Capital Finance Primer** for formula walkthrough and aluminium / power composition.

---

## 4. Issue-tree diagnostic

```
Net Working Capital up year-over-year?
├── Inventory up → price vs. units vs. mix vs. slow movers / spares
├── Accounts Receivable up → sales timing vs. Days Sales Outstanding vs. disputes
└── Accounts Payable down → early pay, volume drop, term regressions
```

---

## 5. Cadence

| Cadence | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| Daily | Accounts Receivable exceptions; critical inventory / fuel |
| Weekly | Cash Conversion Cycle flash; cash bridge |
| Monthly | Full pack and root-cause analysis |
| Quarterly | Board story; scenarios |
| Annually | Policy redesign; five-year plan |

---

## 6. Soft-revenue rule

If Days Inventory Outstanding, Days Sales Outstanding, and Days Payable Outstanding stay constant while revenue falls, **Net Working Capital as % of revenue often rises**. Manage **dollars and percentage** together.

---

## 7. Initiative families

| Lever | Actions | Primary metric |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| Inventory | SKU / spare rationalization; work-in-progress; dead stock; cover-day bands | Days Inventory Outstanding |
| Receivables | Disputes; billing quality; credit; offtaker escalation | Days Sales Outstanding |
| Payables | Term standards; Supply Chain Finance; payment calendar | Days Payable Outstanding |

## 1. Executive summary

This program installs a rigorous, end-to-end way to **measure, explain, and reduce working capital** — cash locked in inventory, customer receivables, and the offset from supplier payables — for a global industrial enterprise.

**Best-practice stance:** mature operators **reduce excess Net Working Capital** and **shorten the Cash Conversion Cycle**. They do **not** inflate working capital without a time-bound strategic reason. Cash released funds capital expenditure, debt reduction, or resilience when revenue is flat or down.

**Decision logic (framework):**

```
Issue  →  Where is cash trapped? (Inventory / Receivables / Payables)
Driver →  Days Inventory Outstanding, Days Sales Outstanding, Days Payable Outstanding
Lever  →  Policy, process, commercial terms, Supply Chain Finance
Guard  →  Service level, generation reliability, supplier health
Target →  Net Working Capital $, Cash Conversion Cycle days, Net Working Capital % of revenue
```

## 2. Business context

### 2.1 Operating profile

- **Scale:** Multi-region industrial footprint (Americas, Europe / Middle East / Africa, Asia-Pacific)
- **Inputs:** Global commodities (metals, energy, chemical feedstocks) — price volatile
- **Reference sectors in scope for composition models:**
  - **Aluminium:** alumina, anodes, metal in process, finished ingots / semis, export receivables
  - **Power:** fuel cover, strategic spares (maintenance, repair, operations), offtaker receivables, unbilled energy
- **Challenge over 5 years:** softer revenue makes static working-capital habits destroy **Free Cash Flow**

### 2.2 Why this analysis is done

| Driver | Business reason |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Cash trapped in operations** | Inventory + Accounts Receivable often exceed Accounts Payable → large positive Net Working Capital |
| **Commodity volatility** | Price spikes inflate inventory value and “just in case” cover days |
| **Revenue soft patch** | With lower sales, Net Working Capital as % of revenue rises unless actively managed |
| **Capital allocation** | Leadership needs cash for Capital Expenditure, optionality, or de-levering |
| **Supplier / customer risk** | Global chains need visibility on Days Payable Outstanding vs. supplier stress; aluminium and power both have strategic counterparties |
| **Single version of truth** | Treasury and Financial Planning & Analysis need shared definitions |

### 2.3 How frequently analysis is run

| Cadence | Scope | Owner |
|---------|--------|--------|
| **Daily** | Accounts Receivable aging alerts; past-due thresholds; inventory / fuel exceptions | Accounts Receivable operations / Inventory control |
| **Weekly** | Cash Conversion Cycle snapshot; cash forecast bridge; top overdue customers or offtakers | Treasury / Credit |
| **Monthly** | Full pack: Days Inventory Outstanding, Days Sales Outstanding, Days Payable Outstanding, Net Working Capital, Net Working Capital % of revenue; region and business-unit drill-down | Financial Planning & Analysis + Business Analyst |
| **Quarterly** | Board pack; initiative return; scenario refresh (base / downside revenue) | Chief Financial Officer office |
| **Annually** | Policy reset (credit terms, payment terms, safety stock / spares); five-year plan | Finance leadership |

## 3. Problem statement

Without a unified working-capital system of insight and action:

1. Leadership sees profit and loss but not **cash conversion** by region, business unit, or commodity.
2. Inventory and spares buffers grow after shocks and never reset.
3. Collections and payment terms are negotiated in silos → weak Cash Conversion Cycle.
4. Under a **five-year lower-revenue** path, static policies erode Free Cash Flow and liquidity ratios.

---

## 4. Goals & success criteria

### 4.1 Primary end goal

> **Reduce Net Working Capital** and **shorten the Cash Conversion Cycle** in a controlled, risk-aware way — releasing cash without breaking production, generation reliability, or strategic supplier relationships.

### 4.2 Target outcomes (full forms)

| Metric (full form) | Baseline (illustrative) | 24-month target | Direction |
|--------------------|-------------------------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Net Working Capital** | $2.40 billion | **$1.95 billion (−$450 million)** | **Reduce** |
| **Net Working Capital as % of revenue** | 22% | **≤ 18%** | **Reduce** (even if revenue softens) |
| **Cash Conversion Cycle** (days) | 78 | **≤ 58** | **Shorten** |
| **Days Inventory Outstanding** | 72 | **≤ 58** | **Reduce** (safer lean) |
| **Days Sales Outstanding** | 52 | **≤ 42** | **Reduce** (faster collect) |
| **Days Payable Outstanding** | 46 | **48–52** (balanced) | **Careful extend** with Supply Chain Finance / supplier health |
| **Cash released** (cumulative) | — | **≥ $400 million** | Maximize |
| Critical service / reliability incidents | Baseline | No deterioration YoY | Hold |

### 4.3 Why “reduce” not “increase” Net Working Capital

| Practice | Guidance |
|----------|----------|
| **Industry best practice** | Minimize Cash Conversion Cycle: lower Days Inventory Outstanding and Days Sales Outstanding; thoughtfully increase Days Payable Outstanding without harming critical suppliers |
| **When an increase is acceptable** | Temporary strategic builds (nearshoring, dual fuel, dual alumina source) — **time-boxed** with exit criteria |
| **This program default** | **Reduce structural Net Working Capital** and improve cash velocity |

## 5. Finance building blocks

### 5.1 Operating formula

```
Net Working Capital
  = Inventory + Accounts Receivable − Accounts Payable
```

```
Cash Conversion Cycle (days)
  = Days Inventory Outstanding
    + Days Sales Outstanding
    − Days Payable Outstanding
```

### 5.2 Composition — aluminium vs power (summary)

| Bucket | Aluminium manufacturing | Power plant / fleet |
|--------|-------------------------|---------------------|
| Inventory | Alumina, anodes, work-in-progress metal, finished ingots / semis, in-transit | Fuel, chemicals, **strategic spares**, outage work-in-progress |
| Accounts Receivable | Industrial and export customers | Offtakers, utilities, power purchase agreements, unbilled energy |
| Accounts Payable | Alumina, power, logistics, maintenance vendors | Fuel, operations & maintenance, contractors |

Full primer (formulas, examples, frameworks): **Working Capital Finance Primer**.

## 9. Key data and key performance indicators

### 9.1 Source systems

Enterprise resource planning; warehouse / manufacturing execution; transport; credit; commodity and fuel price feeds; treasury; sales and operations planning.

### 9.2 Core dictionary (full forms)

| Metric | Formula (standard) | Use |
|--------|--------------------|-----|
| **Inventory** | Raw materials + work-in-progress + finished goods + in-transit (at cost); power: fuel + spares + … | Cash in stock |
| **Accounts Receivable** | Gross trade receivables − allowances (+ unbilled where relevant) | Cash waiting from customers / offtakers |
| **Accounts Payable** | Trade payables | Supplier-financed float |
| **Net Working Capital** | Inventory + Accounts Receivable − Accounts Payable | Primary reduction target |
| **Days Inventory Outstanding** | (Average Inventory / Cost of Goods Sold) × 365 | Inventory velocity |
| **Days Sales Outstanding** | (Average Accounts Receivable / Revenue) × 365 | Collection speed |
| **Days Payable Outstanding** | (Average Accounts Payable / Cost of Goods Sold) × 365 | Payment cycle |
| **Cash Conversion Cycle** | Days Inventory Outstanding + Days Sales Outstanding − Days Payable Outstanding | Days cash is trapped |
| **Net Working Capital % of revenue** | Net Working Capital / Last Twelve Months Revenue | Intensity under revenue decline |
| **Cash released** | Change in Net Working Capital (adjusted) | Program return |
| **Commodity / fuel cover days** | Inventory quantity / daily consumption | Risk vs. capital trade-off |

## 11. Risks & mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|--------|------------|
| Revenue decline inflates Net Working Capital % | High | Dual targets: absolute $ and % of revenue |
| Over-lean inventory → stockout / forced outage | High | Service and reliability floors by asset class |
| Aggressive Days Payable Outstanding harms suppliers | Medium | Supply Chain Finance; segment strategic suppliers |
| Commodity or fuel price spike | High | Cover-day policy bands; valuation bridges |
| Definition disputes | Medium | Single dictionary owned by Financial Planning & Analysis |
| Foreign-exchange noise | Medium | Constant-currency and reported views |

3. Five-year planning includes possible **lower revenue**; working-capital policy must tighten velocity when sales soften.
